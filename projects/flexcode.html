The problem

The work arrives by e-mail. The answers live in the ERP.

A trading business receives customer enquiries, supplier quotes and vendor price lists all day. Turning them into quotations meant retyping items, hunting part numbers and comparing suppliers by hand.

Unstructured input

Items hidden in mail text, PDFs, spreadsheets and photos, in every possible layout.

Exact matching

A very large catalogue where one wrong character in a part number means the wrong product.

Best price, every time

Many suppliers per product, prices changing with each new list.

The flow

From e-mail to draft quotation, automatically.

Moving a card to "Approved for quotation" builds the draft immediately; a background job also checks every few minutes.

Brand intelligence

One brand, many names: aliases are learned, so "PxC" resolves to Phoenix Contact. The AI saves a new alias only when it is confident.

  • Alias and prefix matching
  • Archived brands reactivated, never duplicated
  • One product per manufacturer when part numbers collide

The brand check

If many part numbers in a list already exist under another brand, the import pauses before creating anything:

Same manufacturer → merge the two brands
Products are this brand → move them to it
Different manufacturers → keep separate products

The AI decides alone above a confidence threshold; otherwise it recommends and a person chooses.

Guardrails

AI the business can trust.

The rules that made it safe to run on real customers and real prices.

Exact part numbers onlyNothing invented; a part number written inside other text is still found and re-checked before applying.
Human approvalThe AI drafts; people send, confirm and publish.
Never guess what mattersLead time is never filled by AI, product cost is never changed, unpriced vendors are never auto-selected.
A list only touches its own brandA new price list replaces older prices of the same vendor and brand, never another brand's.
Secrets stay out of chatAPI keys are typed by a person into system settings.
